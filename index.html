Project Context

The project addresses critical regional logistics challenges in Jambi by diverting heavy coal traffic from public roads, mitigating traffic congestion, road degradation, and road safety risks. Consequently, the project balances strategic transportation infrastructure with regional environmental impact mitigation.

Policy Framework & Regulatory Basis

This initiative operates within regional regulatory frameworks concerning dedicated transport infrastructure and environmental conservation:

  • Jambi Provincial Regulation No. 13 of 2012: Regulating coal transportation across Jambi Province and establishing the mandate for dedicated hauling routes.
  • Jambi Provincial Regulation No. 1 of 2015: Governing dedicated road corridors traversing multi-regency administrative boundaries.
  • MoEF (Permen LHK) Regulation No. 4 of 2021: Specifying screening criteria and scale thresholds for mandatory AMDAL and Environmental Management Plans.
  • Government Regulation (PP) No. 22 of 2021: National framework for environmental protection, management, and regulatory approvals.

Translating Policy into Spatial Environmental Assessment

These policy mandates were translated into comprehensive spatial baseline and impact analyses across the proposed corridor:

Policy & Master Plan→Project Alignment→Baseline Environment→Impact Identification→Mitigation & Monitoring→Environmental Feasibility

Hasil Utama

Sungai Progo — Kinerja Paling Stabil: Dengan lebar saluran sekitar 85 m, Progo menunjukkan kinerja model terbaik pada skenario tanpa pemisahan musim (NSE = 0,64 | RMSE = 20,08 m³/s | rRMSE = 41,74%). Temuan Utama

Lebar saluran lebih besar (~85 m)→Proporsi piksel air lebih dominan→Representasi spektral air lebih baik→Estimasi debit lebih stabil
Lebar saluran lebih kecil (≤35 m)→Potensi mixed pixel lebih tinggi→Respons spektral air terpengaruh tutupan sekitar→Estimasi debit kurang stabil

Research Overview

Developed and evaluated a pixel-based river discharge estimation framework utilizing multitemporal Sentinel-2 optical imagery, accounting for river morphology characteristics derived from high-resolution UAV photogrammetry.

The research integrates satellite remote sensing, UAV photogrammetry, GIS analytics, and machine learning to benchmark CMHC method performance across rivers with contrasting channel morphology.

Workflow & Methodology

  • Satellite Image Processing — Google Earth Engine: Processed multitemporal Sentinel-2 imagery including cloud masking, Region of Interest (ROI) extraction, and spectral feature engineering to link optical spectral dynamics with discharge dynamics.
  • UAV Photogrammetry — Agisoft Metashape: Processed DJI Mavic 3 Multispectral aerial drone imagery end-to-end (photo alignment, dense cloud, ground point classification) to generate high-resolution bare-earth DTMs and orthomosaics.
  • Morphological Analysis — ArcGIS: Extracted three micro-channel morphology metrics—channel width, bed slope gradient, and sinuosity—from UAV-derived DTM and orthomosaics.
  • Modeling & Analytics — Python/Google Colab: Implemented Random Forest algorithms for discharge classification alongside Python for preprocessing, threshold optimization, statistical evaluation, and model validation.

Analysis Focus

The study evaluated how effectively Sentinel-2-based CMHC estimates discharge across rivers with varying morphology, and whether seasonal data stratification enhances model performance.

Evaluations across Progo, Oyo, and Serang Rivers revealed that channel width is the most consistent controlling factor governing estimation accuracy, directly tied to pure water pixel proportions vs. mixed pixel contamination at 10 m resolution.

Core Findings

Progo River — Most Robust Performance: With channel width of ~85 m, Progo achieved optimal performance without seasonal stratification (NSE = 0.64 | RMSE = 20.08 m³/s | rRMSE = 41.74%), tracking temporal hydrograph dynamics effectively. Seasonal partitioning lowered model accuracy.

Oyo & Serang Rivers — Spatial Resolution Limits: Performance declined on Oyo (width ~35 m, NSE = -0.04) and Serang (width ~30 m, NSE = -0.37) due to higher mixed pixel proportions from riparian edges at 10 m resolution.

Key Findings

Wider channel (~85 m)→Dominant pure water pixels→Better water spectral signal→More stable discharge estimation
Narrower channel (≤35 m)→Higher mixed pixel contamination→Spectral signal distorted by riparian edge→Less stable estimation

Geospatial Project Value

This project demonstrates end-to-end capabilities in integrating satellite and UAV data, photogrammetric DTM/orthomosaic processing, GIS-based morphological analysis, and developing/evaluating machine learning workflows using Google Earth Engine and Python.
